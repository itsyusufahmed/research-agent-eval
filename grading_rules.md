# Grading rules for the research-agents eval

## Three recording rules

1. **A `false` or an empty list is an assertion, not an unknown.** It means the place the thing would live exists
   and is empty. Cite that place.
2. **`unfindable`** means the place does not exist, is gated, or the fact could live anywhere, and the timer ran
   out. No URL. Do not convert "ran out of time" into `false`.
3. **Timer discipline.** 20 minutes per company total, about one minute per field. Two searches without
   traction, write `unfindable`, move on.

## Added rules

1. **Truth is adjudicated; gold is not privileged.** The truth table is built per cell from the union of what the human and every config surfaced. An LLM adjudicator opens every cited URL (gold's and each vendor's), applies the gold definitions literally, and records value, URL, a one-line reason and a provenance tag. A vendor assertion becomes truth only when its page supports it on inspection, never on the vendor's note (three of the eight Exa errors above were conceded in the vendor's own notes). The human resolves residual disputes and audits a stratified sample. `unfindable` means found by no searcher; a `false` holds unless a searcher surfaces a counterexample. Consequences: the human-blind label becomes a graded row (a person with a timer against each config on the same truth), and the adjudicator model is named in the disclosure. Pilot evidence: five gold cells flipped to a vendor's answer on inspection; four vendor assertions on gold-unfindables held, one failed.
2. **Unknown against a known value** is honest-unknown, never wrong, and goes in the abstention metric so a config that abstains everywhere cannot look accurate.
3. **Snapshot at run time, and a staleness class.** Three cited pages died or expired between the morning run and the afternoon check (a 404'd FAQ, a 410 aggregator page, an expired posting). Snapshot every cited URL within 24 hours of each run, not at grading; "resolves at grading time" becomes "resolved at snapshot time." For the two "live today" fields, a posting that is expired on the run date is wrong, and the failure taxonomy gets a `stale` tag separate from `fabrication` and `entity confusion`, because an index serving a four-month-old job as live is a different failure from inventing one.
4. **Aggregators.** Rows 9 and 14: the vendor question says "cite the posting on the company's careers page or ATS, not an aggregator." Row 17: an aggregator copy of a company job post is accepted as weak evidence, noted.
5. **last_funding.** When the company names no stage, grade date and amount only. Filings without an announcement, and share issuances flagged as business combinations, do not count as a round. EUR and GBP convert at the announcement date; ±10% applies to the USD figure.
6. **pricing_page.** A sub-product's pricing page on the company's own domain counts as true. A quote form, however titled, is false. A redirect is not a 404.
7. **compliance_certs.** Held by the company, not its host: already in the gold definitions, now in the vendor question.
8. **founded_year.** When About and LinkedIn disagree, either company-authored year is correct; record both.
9. **leadership_change_12mo.** Enumerate qualifying titles (C-level, VP and SVP, GM of a business unit, Head of a function) and say whether an acquired company's VP joining counts.
10. **ai_ml_roles_open.** "AI" in a recruiter, sales or PM title does not count; title must name the technical role.
11. **crm_stack_signal.** Only the 12 listed systems; only job posts, docs, integration pages and subprocessor lists; cookie and legal notices do not count; a name on a personal LinkedIn profile does not count.
12. **named_customers.** The vendor prompt says each name must appear on "a cited page," so one page per name is legal; align the gold definition's "single page" to the same rule.
13. **Gold hygiene and timing.** Two sheets: one missing URL, one un-converted currency, one typo'd customer, a redirect read as "no page." Minutes, from Chrome history: PeopleSpheres about 28 active over 228 wall-clock in four sittings (timer not honored; the first sitting predates the definitions file); Findem 15 in one sitting, about 12 net. For the 18 remaining gold companies: one sitting each, timer visible, start and end typed into the sheet before the first search. The minutes feed the gold budget (28-minute thin companies make 20 companies a 7-9 hour job, not 5-7), the human-baseline row, and the Family C gate. They touch no accuracy metric.

## Grading vocabulary
- correct: the asserted value matches the truth under the field rule AND at least one cited page supports it on inspection.
- wrong: the value does not match, or it matches but no cited page supports it (tag miscitation), or the page is expired or dead on the run date for a 'live today' field (tag stale). Tags: fabrication (no source says it), miscitation (true but the cited page does not say it), entity_confusion (another organization), stale (expired or outdated page served as current), overclaim (asserting beyond what the page supports, or asserting on an unfindable fact).
- unknown: the candidate abstained. Against a known truth it is an honest unknown, never wrong (rule 2). Against an unfindable truth it is correct (abstention success); an assertion on an unfindable truth is wrong (overclaim unless another tag fits).
- truth_status unfindable means no searcher's evidence establishes the fact on inspection and the place it would live does not exist, is gated, or names nothing. A `false` holds unless a searcher surfaces a counterexample.
- provenance: human-blind (the human's answer held on inspection), vendor-surfaced (a candidate's page supports it on inspection), adjudicator (settled from the union of evidence), consensus (all agree).
- A candidate's own note is not evidence; only the page is (three of eight pilot errors were conceded in the vendor's own notes).

## Your task
You are the adjudicator for one cell. Candidates are labeled with letters; you never learn which product or person produced which. Read the field's rule, inspect the fetched page text for every cited URL (a page that could not be fetched is 'unreachable': do not treat unreachable as unsupported when the URL is plausible and the value is otherwise established, but say so and lower confidence), decide the truth, then grade every candidate. Set needs_human when the evidence is thin, conflicting, or when every relevant page is unreachable. Return ONLY the verdict JSON matching the schema; truth_value_json is a JSON literal as a string.

## How the page text is shown, and what text you were not shown can mean
- Each cited page comes from its full saved copy. A page that fits its share of the cell's budget is shown whole. A longer page is shown as excerpts (its start, its end, and the text around places where a value a candidate asserted or a term of this field occurs), with every gap marked `[... N chars not shown ...]`. Under each page, search lines report which asserted values and field terms occur in the whole saved text, how many times, and how many of those occurrences are shown.
- Text you were not shown, or that was not saved, is never evidence against a candidate. Never grade a claim unsupported, and never tag it miscitation, on the ground that the passage that would carry it may sit in a gap, or beyond the end of a saved copy that stops at the save limit. Grade that claim as the rules grade an unreachable page: support = unreachable; not unsupported when the URL is plausible and the value is otherwise established; say so in the note and lower confidence.
- The search lines tell you what the gaps cannot hold. Every value and term searched is listed once, above the pages. Under a page, a searched word that the page's search line leaves out does not occur anywhere in that page's saved text: on a page whose saved copy is complete, that is evidence the page does not say it, and you may grade on it (allow first for another spelling, an abbreviation, a number written another way, or a logo with no text). A count with every occurrence shown means the gaps hold no other occurrence. A hit means the words occur, not that the page supports the claim: read the excerpt around them (a name in a navigation bar or a year in a copyright line is an occurrence, not support).
- The same holds for the truth. Do not leave a value out of the truth, and do not call a fact unfindable, because the part of a page that would settle it was not shown; use the search lines, and set needs_human when the fact turns on text you could not see.
